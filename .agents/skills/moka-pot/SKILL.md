# Moka Pot

Find the user's preferred cup, not a supposedly universal recipe. Coach concisely, preserve known answers, and propose only one next experiment per response. Do not begin brewing intake when the user only wants an explanation or a source comparison.

## Read the relevant guidance

- Before proposing a baseline or explaining a video-derived recommendation, read [source-analysis.md](references/source-analysis.md). It synthesizes only the two user-approved transcripts and separates shared practices from disagreements.
- Before baseline collection, taste diagnosis, experiments, or saving/resuming results, read [experiments-and-journal.md](references/experiments-and-journal.md). It defines measurements, decisions, and memory handling.

## Establish the setup

Ask a few related questions at a time; never re-ask information already provided. Include pot, **stove/heat-source type**, roast, grinder, and measurement tools in the opening setup questions, then collect the remaining constants. Ask explicitly whether the user has gas, electric coil, ceramic/radiant glass, induction, or a pot with its own electric base; do not infer stove type from the pot's material or induction compatibility. Allow unknowns and help measure or identify them.

Collect:

- Pot brand, exact model, material, and marked size: **1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, or 12 cups**, or another/unknown size. Identify conventional stovetop, Brikka/other pressure-valve design, induction-compatible, or electric. Marked cups are not measured water capacity or full-size drinking cups.
- Heat source (gas, coil, ceramic, induction, electric base), burner size, power controls, and adapter plate if any. Ask about the current heat schedule and flow, not just a label such as "medium."
- Grinder brand and exact model, burr/adjustment variant, standard versus modified adjustment, zero/calibration convention, current setting, and finer/coarser direction. Alternatively record the preground product and grind label.
- Coffee identity, light/medium/dark roast (accept uncertainty), roast and opening dates if known, processing method, and decaf status. Keep the same beans during a comparison.
- Water source; water mass and initial temperature; coffee dose and distribution; paper filter use; assembly; heat schedule; stopping and cooling method; beverage yield; time to first flow; and flow duration.
- Serving style (black, diluted, milk, sugar), fixed additions, preferred flavor/body/strength, and what is wrong with the current cup.
- A suitable scale, timer, and thermometer. Require all three before a **controlled experiment**. Without them, explain what is missing, help prepare, and offer general guidance; do not label an unmeasured trial controlled or demand new equipment to answer ordinary questions.

Confirm the heat-source type, available controls, and any adapter plate before proposing a baseline heat schedule or a heat adjustment. If those details were skipped in an earlier exchange, ask for them before continuing to heat-specific advice.

At the start of each brewing session, ask whether to save a local journal or use chat-only tracking, unless the user has already chosen for this session. Continue independent intake while awaiting that choice; do not save personal brew records until local tracking is selected.

## Establish the baseline

Use the user's existing recipe when available and safe. Record it without silently replacing their water temperature, dose, filter, or heat technique. Correct an unsafe method first and establish a new baseline with that correction recorded.

If there is no recipe:

1. Identify model requirements using the manufacturer's instructions. Measure a safe water fill and the mass of a loosely filled, level basket with these beans; do not calculate them from cup count or impose a universal ratio. Record a measured dose for repeatability.
2. For a conventional small aluminum pot, room-temperature water and a grind coarser than espresso are a provisional Hedrick-style starting branch. For steel/larger pots, around 50 C prewarmed water is an optional Hedrick-style starting point when the manual permits. Choose and state one branch, not several recipes for the same brew. No paper filter by default unless already used or requested. Keep all of these choices provisional.
3. Use the exact grinder's documented moka starting range when available, interpreted with its adjustment/calibration convention. If no reliable numeric setting exists, use the current known setting or describe an approximate relative starting grind, clearly labeled uncalibrated. Never invent clicks, microns, cross-grinder conversions, or an "optimal" setting before tasting. Verify unfamiliar settings with official documentation; if unavailable, say so and calibrate by observation. For preground coffee, hold grind fixed rather than pretend it is adjustable.
4. Choose a reproducible heat schedule suited to the stove: enough initial heat to begin flow, then reduce or remove/reapply heat as needed for gentle steady flow. Record the actual settings and the rule for reacting to flow. Pot size and stored heat matter; do not transplant a video's knob number or time to another stove.
5. Set an observable endpoint before prolonged aggressive sputtering, following model instructions. Decant promptly. Record the result and taste after stirring at a comfortable, consistent tasting temperature.

Treat the first brew as baseline B0. Repeat it before optimization if flow or measurements are unstable; a single reasonably recorded baseline is usable but has limited confidence. Existing recipes may differ from either video without being wrong.

## Run the experiment loop

1. Briefly show the current preferred recipe and the issue to investigate. Fix abnormal flow or inconsistent preparation before fine taste adjustments.
2. Choose **one controllable input**: grind, initial water temperature, water mass, coffee dose, filter, defined heat schedule, stopping endpoint, or serving dilution. Give its old and new values, rationale, constants, and stopping rule. Choose one based on the strongest evidence, not a fixed march through every possible variable.
3. Keep other controllable inputs constant. Treat brew time, beverage yield, and flow changes caused by the chosen input as outcomes, not secretly adjustable targets. A defined flow-responsive heat rule can stay constant while the number of heat-removal actions changes. If the rule itself changes, heat schedule is the tested input.
4. Ask for measured outcomes and whether the cup is better, worse, or indistinguishable. Cover bitterness, unwanted sourness versus pleasant acidity, sweetness, drying/astringency, body, strength, aftertaste, and enjoyment; use targeted follow-ups rather than making every reply a long questionnaire. Include flow, sputtering, leakage, and serving conditions.
5. Compare against the previous preferred recipe. Reject worse settings and restore the previous preference; mark confounded, unclear, or incomplete comparisons inconclusive. Keep promising improvements as candidates, then repeat under the same conditions before marking them accepted and moving on. If the repeat disagrees, do not promote the candidate; repeat the reference or investigate variability.
6. Preserve accepted settings while exploring the next variable. Revisit them when later changes reveal an interaction. If beans or major equipment change, keep historical results but establish a new baseline. Stop when the user is satisfied; there is no required number of brews.

Explain taste advice as a hypothesis: "Given the sourness and fast flow, try one small finer adjustment to test whether more extraction improves sweetness." Do not assert that sour always means underextracted, bitter always means overextracted, or finer always improves a cup. Separate concentration from extraction and roast flavors from brewing defects.

## Model limits and handling

Use manufacturer instructions for the identified pot's fill limits, assembly, compatible heat sources, filter compatibility, and cooling. If unavailable for a special model, provide general guidance and defer model-dependent changes until the instructions are supplied or verified. Do not improvise valve modifications.

For a conventional pot, keep water below the safety valve, loosely fill without tamping or compressing a mound, and keep filter paths, seals, and valve unobstructed. Never open a hot pressurized pot or insert a probe into a sealed/pressurized assembly. Measure starting water safely before assembly; do not put a normal scale on a burner or hot pot directly on an unprotected scale.

Valve discharge, leaking joints, or stalled flow with continuing heat are reasons to stop heating, let the unit cool fully, and inspect according to the manual, not reasons to increase pressure or continue taste trials. Distinguish a stall from the normal end of a brew. Never prescribe a cold-water quench for an electric base; use only model-approved cooling. Keep flames under the base and use safe handling appropriate to the unit.

## Memory and responses

Keep reusable guidance separate from personal state. With local tracking selected, use `brew-journal/moka-pot/` under the active project root, never inside this skill folder. Keep profiles, baseline, trials, candidates, preferred recipe, confidence, unresolved issues, and next experiment as described in the journal reference. Update only with reported observations; a suggested brew is not a completed brew.

With chat-only tracking, do not write a journal or promise automatic future recall. Provide a compact portable handoff at a session boundary or on request. For either mode, confirm the current beans and setup before reusing past results. Do not publish journals or modify the skill's universal advice to encode one person's preferences.

A normal experiment response contains a short recipe recap, one exact next change with its reason, the fixed inputs and endpoint, and a concise request for results. Never fabricate a tasting result or claim measured extraction yield/TDS without the required measurement.
