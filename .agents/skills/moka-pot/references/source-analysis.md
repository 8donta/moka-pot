## Disagreements to preserve as alternatives

### Starting water temperature

**H:** Recommends freshly boiled water added to a cooler base, reporting an initial temperature in the low 90s C for his setup. He also cautions against beginning with the assembled boiler at near-boiling conditions when water was boiled in it. This is not an exact starting temperature guaranteed by every pot.

**L:** Recommends room-temperature water for small aluminum pots; suggests around 50 C prewarmed water for steel and potentially warmer starts for larger pots to reduce waiting. Demonstrates higher chimney-output temperatures and a less preferred taste with the preboiled comparison in his setup.

**Coaching consequence:** Neither is a universal constant. Choose one measured starting branch and compare starting water temperature as its own input, with water mass, grind, heat-control rule, and stopping rule unchanged. Let heat-up time and beverage yield vary and record them. Repeat the preferred result. Do not switch grind and water temperature together to compare the creators' complete recipes and call that a temperature test.

### Grind

**H:** For his relatively light roast, finer than filter coffee but clearly coarser than espresso; seeks a fine grind that still permits a good yield without overheating.

**L:** Generally coarser than conventional moka recommendations, around a typical V60 reference, paired with active flow management. Clarifies that his own V60 grind is unusually coarse and that this is not the intended moka reference. Suggests going somewhat finer if his starting point tastes too sour or flows too fast.

**Coaching consequence:** These relative descriptions overlap and are not calibrated particle sizes. Neither transcript supplies transferable numerical grinder settings. Ask about exact hardware, reference setting, and calibration; verify documentation before proposing clicks or dial positions. Label a new setting a starting point or trial, not optimal.

### Ratios and water fill

**H:** Describes approximately 1:10 coffee-to-input-water as a common starting relationship for a full basket and boiler below the valve, with substantial variation by pot and roast. His example uses about 30 g coffee and roughly 280 g input water, yielding about 200 g. For darker roasts he discusses less water and lower beverage yield.

**L:** Discusses shorter input ratios for concentration. In an illustrative light-roast comparison, corrects an earlier statement and uses 21 g coffee with 105 g water (1:5 input), hoping for around 1:3 coffee-to-beverage output. Later demonstrates a simpler roughly full-basket, below-valve approach. He does not require the short ratio for every pot.

**Coaching consequence:** Distinguish coffee:input-water from coffee:beverage-output ratios. These examples are not capacity tables. Lower boiler fill changes headspace and thermal behavior as well as available yield, so a water-mass trial does not isolate concentration alone. Never change water mass automatically to preserve a ratio during a dose experiment. Follow the actual model's allowable fill range.

### Brew endpoint and time

**H:** Maximizes useful liquid before aggressive sputtering, particularly for light roasts. His approximate two-thirds input-to-output observation is specific guidance, not a mandatory yield target for all pots.

**L:** Uses roughly 1 g/s as a visual flow reference, without direct continuous weighing. Finds a little controlled end bubbling tolerable but prefers excluding a prolonged harsh tail. His rough time-to-first-flow suggestions vary by pot size and stove.

**Coaching consequence:** Do not mandate 1 g/s, a percentage yield, or one flow duration. Define whether the tested input is a heat schedule or an endpoint. A stopwatch reading is an outcome, not an independently adjustable machine setting. Do not place a pot on a scale while heating it to imitate a live flow measurement.

## Temperature and extraction claims

L measures near the upper outlet and explicitly distinguishes that location from the hotter coffee basket. His suggested basket temperatures are estimates, not direct readings. Neither an initial water temperature nor an outlet temperature proves the coffee-bed temperature. Do not ask a home user to instrument or modify a sealed pot to reproduce the demonstrations.

The transcripts support testing taste, flow, time, and beverage mass. They do not establish the user's measured extraction percentage, TDS, caffeine yield, a universal pressure limit, or a universal chemical explanation for a taste. Use mechanisms as tentative explanations unless independently verified for a question requiring that precision.

## Model boundaries

The demonstrations primarily concern conventional Bialetti-style pots, including aluminum and steel. Extend principles cautiously to other cup sizes and heat sources. Brikka and other valve-controlled designs, induction plates, and electric units require their own manufacturer's instructions. Do not prescribe filters, reduced fills, heat cycling, open-lid operation, or water cooling to a special model merely because a conventional pot demonstrated it. Manufacturer requirements override video technique where they conflict.
